# Phase 1 presentation: ten-slide detailed speaker guide

The PowerPoint contains the same notes. Aim for 12–15 minutes; all errors and concentrations are in µg/m³. Do not describe R² as percentage accuracy or the historical demo as live air quality. No individual contribution split has been invented.

## 1. Research question and complete Phase 1 scope

We estimate the observed PM2.5 concentration one hour ahead. After receiving the 10:00 reading, predict 11:00 using pollution and measured weather available through 10:00. Future calendar values are known, but measured target-hour weather is not. The output is concentration in µg/m³. The question is the incremental benefit beyond persistence, not whether a complex model can achieve a large R². Phase 1 delivered literature review, a validated hourly archive, training-only EDA, causal feature engineering, persistence and daily seasonal naive, Ridge and XGBoost, chronological selection, rolling holdout backtests, diagnostics, saved inference, notebooks/CLI, Aircast, tests and documentation. The figures in this deck are built from the completed real-data run, not synthetic fixtures. Separate deep-learning or hybrid modules do not add results to the Phase 1 tables. We will show both gains and limitations, including the strong validation baselines and the difficult high-pollution subset.

## 2. Dataset, gaps and cleaning decisions

The original UCI 381 file has 43,824 hourly rows, sorted unique timestamps, no absent timestamp rows and 2,067 missing PM2.5 measurements (4.72%). Numeric weather is complete. The gap audit finds 214 concentration runs, with a maximum length of 155 hours. We leave missing measurements in their hourly slots: deleting the 10:00 row would incorrectly make a one-row shift at 11:00 point to 09:00. The loader validates dates, numeric fields and duplicate timestamps, sorts and regularizes before features are built, and removes the No identifier. No target interpolation is used for scoring. Valid high concentrations remain: a statistical outlier is not automatically a recording error. The histogram uses only 2010–2012, with 24,418 measured values, mean 97.82, median 73, range 0–994, skewness 1.70 and p90 218 µg/m³. The missingness plot is a descriptive full-archive quality audit, not an EDA-based predictor decision using holdout outcomes. Missing counts are 669, 728, 489, 82 and 99 in 2010–2014 respectively. Pollution was recorded at the US Embassy and weather at Beijing Capital International Airport, introducing a spatial mismatch. Dataset: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/381/beijing+pm2+5+data ; DOI 10.24432/C5JS49, CC BY 4.0. Environmental context: Liang et al. (2015), DOI 10.1098/rspa.2015.0257.

## 3. EDA interpretation and why the features are plausible

The lag autocorrelations are measured on the regular hourly training grid without deleting gaps. One-hour autocorrelation is 0.961, 24-hour is 0.394 and 168-hour is approximately −0.006. Strong short-term dependence motivates persistence and recent lags. It does not prove each proposed lag is useful; grouped ablation later tests additions. The hourly graph pools the 2010–2012 values by hour of day. The highest mean is at 01:00 (113.08, based on 1,020 observations). October has the largest monthly mean (118.17) in the saved training profile, though the deck focuses on the daily graph for readability. Calendar features are candidates, not a claim that every day repeats yesterday. Numeric weather correlations with PM2.5 include wind speed −0.264, dew point +0.245, pressure −0.138, rain hours −0.055, snow hours +0.011 and temperature +0.003. Small Pearson correlations do not exclude nonlinear predictive effects. Spatial mismatch and correlated weather variables prevent a causal interpretation. The right tail and 7.17% training missingness motivate retaining peaks and reporting historical support. These findings justify features and diagnostics before inspecting the holdout, while chronological validation determines whether additions improve forecasts.

## 4. Feature accounting, lag zero and model theory

The ten PM2.5 offsets are 0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, 168, each with a missingness flag (20 columns). Lag zero is the current observed y(t), not the future label. Six trailing windows each supply mean, population standard deviation (ddof 0) and observed fraction (18). Missing summaries remain missing when the support is empty. Seven calendar columns use sine/cosine for known target hour, weekday and month plus weekend. Six numeric weather fields at 0, 1 and 24 hours contribute 18 columns, current weather missing flags contribute six, and current wind contributes one categorical column. Total 70; four observed wind categories give 73 encoded columns. Encoders and Ridge medians/scalers are learned inside each fold. Persistence repeats y(t); the seasonal source is t−23, which is one day before the target t+1. Using t−24 would be the wrong clock hour. Ridge fits squared error plus an L2 penalty with alpha 10. XGBoost builds F_K(x)=b+sum eta f_k(x), penalizing tree leaves and leaf weights; gradients/Hessians yield leaf score −G/(H+lambda). Depth, child weight, shrinkage, sampling and regularization control complexity. Learned outputs are clipped at zero consistently in all phases. Sources: Chen and Guestrin (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02754 ; Hoerl and Kennard (1970), DOI 10.1080/00401706.1970.10488634.

## 5. Time splits, parameter choice, ablation and fair coverage

The 2010–2012 period is initial training and EDA. Four expanding quarterly folds in 2013 choose parameters using pooled validation RMSE. Four quarterly 2014 folds then evaluate the frozen setting. The figure shows XGBoost validation RMSE falling 27.513 → 27.092 → 26.772 as trailing/calendar and weather groups are added. The selected settings remain fixed during this comparison; it is not separate optimal tuning for every group. Dashed reference lines show persistence 24.870 and Ridge 23.809, both better than full XGBoost on validation. Candidate RMSEs are 26.772, 26.801, 26.953, a narrow separation rather than a globally proven optimum. Selected XGBoost has 300 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.05, child weight 5, subsample 0.8, colsample 0.9 and lambda 5, with seed 42, histogram trees and two threads. There is no test-based early stopping. For block start B, the first forecast origin is B−h and every training label must be known by that origin. Q1 2014 uses labels through 31 December 2013 at 23:00, with 32,951 eligible training rows; Q4 has 39,447. Later observed quarters can enter later fits under the same policy. The shared mask requires warm-up, a real target and both naive inputs, yielding 8,563 validation and 8,531 holdout targets. Exclusion counts overlap. Methodological basis: https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html

## 6. Pooled results, metrics and interpretation

All four methods are scored on the same 8,531 observed target hours out of 8,760 in 2014: 97.39% coverage. The 229 excluded hours lack a target or at least one naive input; the exclusion counts overlap. The graph starts at zero and shows the weak seasonal reference without hiding its large error. Persistence MAE 11.926, RMSE 22.033 and R² 0.9442; daily seasonal naive 67.296, 98.754 and −0.1205; Ridge 11.825, 21.063 and 0.9490; XGBoost 11.833, 21.088 and 0.9489. XGBoost improves RMSE 4.29% over persistence, while Ridge improves 4.41%. The learned-model gap of about 0.025 is not a tested statistically significant difference. MAE averages absolute errors; RMSE square-roots the mean squared error and emphasizes large misses. R² compares squared errors with the observed-period mean denominator, may be negative and must not be called percentage accuracy. The negative seasonal R² is retained. Pooled metrics use all errors rather than averaged quarterly RMSEs. XGBoost's favorable 2014 comparison does not erase its weaker 2013 validation comparison. Historical point errors do not give calibrated intervals for future predictions.

## 7. Quarterly variation and pollution regimes

Quarterly RMSE makes temporal variation visible. Persistence is 27.298, 22.208, 14.527 and 22.255; Ridge 26.063, 21.311, 14.012 and 21.148; XGBoost 26.839, 20.758, 14.087 and 20.770. Both learned methods beat persistence on RMSE each quarter, but Ridge leads Q1 and Q3 and XGBoost Q2 and Q4. XGBoost's Q1 MAE is worse than persistence despite the smaller RMSE. We fix the high-pollution threshold at 218 using the 2010–2012 p90, without consulting the holdout distribution. There are 865 high and 7,666 lower-pollution common hours. On high hours, persistence RMSE is 38.557, Ridge 38.522 and XGBoost 39.959. Below 218, errors are 19.301, 18.062 and 17.740, so the pooled XGBoost gain is largely associated with the lower-pollution subset. High-pollution bias is −5.534 for persistence, −13.313 for Ridge and −11.912 for XGBoost. Negative bias means underprediction. These are descriptive subsets, not a new optimized experiment. No significant ranking or causal mechanism is inferred from the differences. A practical peak-focused use case needs a separately evaluated peak-error objective.

## 8. Forecast trace, error distribution and abrupt spike

The upper graph plots every common forecast hour in the final seven days of 2014, with missing scored hours represented as breaks. Observed concentration, Ridge and XGBoost share the same timestamps. These are saved out-of-fold predictions made by quarterly fitted models, not the final archive-trained models. Tracking the recent level does not guarantee anticipating a sharp change. The lower-left graph uses signed residuals prediction minus observation from all 8,531 holdout hours. Its display is restricted to ±80 µg/m³ for legibility: 78 XGBoost and 75 Ridge errors lie outside this view; histogram percentages retain the denominator of all 8,531 hours. Thus the tails are not silently removed from metrics. Negative residuals are underpredictions. XGBoost median absolute error is 6.88, p90 26.28 and p95 37.46; these empirical quantiles are not prediction intervals. One-hour residual autocorrelation is 0.120 for XGBoost and 0.063 for Ridge, so residual structure remains. The lower-right graph is the largest XGBoost miss, chosen explicitly as a failure case. PM2.5 rises from 80 to 580 in one hour; persistence predicts 80, Ridge 92.469, XGBoost 86.801, an XGBoost absolute miss of about 493.199. Without an independent sensor-quality flag, we retain this target. The visual example explains a failure mode rather than proving all peaks have one cause.

## 9. Aircast, gain importance, audits and future work

Aircast lets a user append a manual reading or upload an hourly CSV, select an available model, inspect history, generate a next-hour forecast, export results and open the report. The frontend is HTML/CSS/JavaScript with an SVG chart, and Flask validates data before invoking the original feature builder. Uploads need at least 169 hourly slots, unique valid timestamps and physical numeric values, and are bounded at 4 MB / 100,000 rows or hourly slots. The normalized recent window travels with each request and is revalidated, so different server instances can forecast without shared upload sessions. Final model artifacts are refitted only after evaluation and reject histories ending before their fit endpoint. Gain importance belongs to that final refit: latest PM2.5 51.1% and three-hour mean 24.5%; it is not causal attribution, a stability study or holdout feature selection. The recorded independent audit refitted both learned methods across four holdout quarters, reproducing all 17,062 predictions with maximum floating-point difference below 6e−14. It also checked actual targets, references, recomputed metrics, manual-input response and stateless upload parity. Data/source hashes, configuration, versions, selection, fold audits, predictions, model artifacts, preparation exports and tests remain inspectable. Synthetic fixtures test behavior and do not supply scores. Vercel reported Ready; the existing record does not independently verify hosted inference or browser rendering. Sample data is historical. The original uploaded archive gives 2015-01-01 00:00 predictions XGBoost 11.92273 and Ridge 13.63978, unscored because the target is outside the archive. No AQI mapping or calibrated prediction interval is supplied. Future peak objectives, uncertainty methods and sequence/hybrid models require fresh evaluation or an exploratory label after inspecting 2014.
